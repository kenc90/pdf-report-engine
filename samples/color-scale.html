Color scales

colorScale / data-color-scale paints a cell background by mapping its number onto a two-colour ramp: from at the minimum, to at the maximum, a blend in between. Every cell gets one flat fill — the ramp is per value, not a painted gradient — and dark fills switch their text to white automatically.

1. Auto and explicit ranges

Revenue and margin derive their bounds per column from the rows actually printed. CSAT uses an explicit 0–100 domain, so its colours stay comparable when the data changes.

Region Revenue Margin CSAT

Bounds are computed across the detail rows of that column. Cells holding a non-numeric value get no fill.

2. The ramp, one cell at a time

The same declaration with an explicit 0–100 domain on six cells. This is the whole mechanism: each value becomes one interpolated colour.

0 20 40 60 80 100

Adjacent cells look like a gradient because the RGB blend is continuous; each cell itself is a single solid fill. Cells near the dark end have already flipped to white text.

3. Text colour behaviour

The scale controls the background. Text turns white only when the computed fill is dark; text=false keeps the cell's own colour so you can choose it yourself.

default (auto white) text=false (keeps dark) text=false + white style
95 95 95

An explicit cell background, or a matching data-when-style background, wins over the scale's fill. The scale supplies a default background, not an override.
